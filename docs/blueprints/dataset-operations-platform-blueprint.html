How To Read This Blueprint

This document translates the Caudals B2B pivot into a concrete platform design. It assumes the near-term product is a high-touch managed dataset operations service with a public landing page, contact form, blog, and private admin dashboard. It does not recommend exposing public marketplace browse, buyer self-service, supplier portals, checkout, or individual sample uploads in the current phase.

Primary Answer

Build Caudals as a Dataset Operations OS: a private control plane that turns buyer demand and supplier data assets into versioned, documented, legally cleared, QA-scored, ML-ready dataset releases.

Key Product Rule

Treat every dataset as a product release, not a file export. A release is only complete when data artifacts, rights evidence, privacy assessment, lineage, schema, quality score, documentation, pricing, and delivery evidence exist together.

01

Executive Blueprint

Caudals should prioritize the internal system that makes high-trust dataset delivery repeatable. Marketplace exposure can wait; the operational control plane is the current product.

What Buyers Need

Custom or hard-to-access data that is usable for model training, fine-tuning, evaluation, benchmarking, enrichment, or RAG.

  • Clear permitted use and AI-training rights.
  • Fast feasibility decisions and samples.
  • ML-ready packaging and documentation.
  • Quality, limitations, and lineage evidence.

What Suppliers Need

A managed path to monetize proprietary data without building sales, privacy, packaging, delivery, and buyer-support operations.

  • Safe rights and sensitivity review.
  • Low-friction data-room intake.
  • Revenue share and listing readiness.
  • Controls over what is exposed or sold.

What Caudals Needs

An operator-grade platform for sourcing, rights review, ingestion, profiling, cleaning, anonymization, labeling, QA, packaging, and delivery.

  • Auditable state transitions.
  • Reusable build pipelines.
  • Dataset-level unit economics.
  • Visible blockers and acceptance gates.

Strategic Architecture Decision

Decision Recommendation Rationale
Product surface Private admin dashboard first. Early revenue depends on managed delivery quality, not self-service marketplace breadth.
Data plane Object storage with staged zones: raw, profiled, cleaned, labeled, packaged, delivered. Supports large files, multimodal assets, immutable raw preservation, and buyer-specific package outputs.
Control plane Supabase Postgres metadata, RLS, audit log, and server actions/API routes. Fits current stack while enforcing scoped access and operational traceability.
Workflow engine Start with job tables and workers, then move core builds into Dagster asset orchestration. Dagster's asset model fits dataset products and makes lineage, observability, and re-materialization first-class.
Dataset standards Ship Dataset Cards, Croissant JSON-LD manifests, SPDX license identifiers, and W3C PROV-inspired lineage. Improves buyer trust, ML tool compatibility, search/catalog readiness, and reproducibility.
Quality gates No release without rights, privacy, schema, quality, label, packaging, and delivery approvals. Prevents the platform's highest-risk failure modes: illegal listing, PII leakage, low-quality delivery, and unclear buyer use rights.

Near-Term Product

  • Lead triage for buyer demand and supplier monetization interest.
  • Structured buyer briefs and supplier asset records.
  • Dataset build board with stages, owners, blockers, and gates.
  • Pipeline run registry for ingestion, profiling, cleaning, PII, labeling, QA, and packaging.
  • Release package generator with manifests, quality report, dataset card, and delivery evidence.

Explicit Non-Goals

  • No individual sample-upload workflow.
  • No public buyer workspace until requirements, pricing, and fulfillment are redesigned.
  • No supplier portal until rights capture and source access flows are safe.
  • No public marketplace catalog until every listing has rights, privacy, provenance, sample, QA, and refresh evidence.
  • No payment relaunch until offer, license, delivery, and supplier-share records are reliable.

Platform Principles

The platform should be designed around trust, repeatability, and operational throughput. The first clients will judge Caudals by whether datasets arrive usable, documented, licensed, and on time.

Dataset Product Contract

Every sellable dataset version must have:

  • Commercial purpose and buyer use case.
  • Supplier/source provenance and permitted-use evidence.
  • Privacy classification, PII handling decision, and residual risk.
  • Data dictionary, schema, modality profile, and format profile.
  • Quality scorecard with method, sample size, checks, failures, and known limits.
  • Release manifest with checksums, row/object counts, splits, packaging formats, and artifact paths.
  • Dataset card and buyer-facing delivery note.

Operational Invariants

  • Raw supplier data is immutable after ingestion; later transformations create new artifacts.
  • Every stage emits metadata, not just files.
  • Every human decision affecting rights, PII, pricing, publication, or delivery is audit logged.
  • Pipeline runs are idempotent where possible and restartable where not.
  • Buyer-visible materials never imply permissions that are not explicitly cleared.
  • Internal admin remains the control room; public marketplace surfaces stay hidden until redesigned.

Required Stage Gates

Gate Required Before Evidence Owner
Rights Gate Any external sample, listing, or delivery. Source owner, contract, permitted AI use, redistribution limits, prohibited uses. Operator + legal reviewer
Privacy Gate Cleaning, labeling vendor export, buyer sample, or buyer release. PII scan, sensitivity class, minimization decision, anonymization/pseudonymization method, residual risk. Data engineer + privacy reviewer
Quality Gate Package release approval. Expectations, profile deltas, duplicate rate, missingness, label quality, split leakage checks. Data science lead
Documentation Gate Buyer handoff or catalog-ready status. Dataset card, data dictionary, release manifest, limitations, usage notes. Dataset operator
Delivery Gate Signed download, S3 share, API, warehouse share, or private transfer. License, invoice/quote state if applicable, checksum, delivery log, acceptance criteria. Commercial ops + operator
Blueprint posture The platform should make the unsafe path hard. Operators can move quickly, but the UI and backend should refuse publication or buyer delivery when required rights, PII, QA, documentation, or license records are missing.

Technology Stack

Keep the existing web stack, add a Python data operations plane, and delay heavyweight lakehouse complexity until data volume, concurrency, and client requirements justify it.

Recommended Stack By Layer

Layer MVP Choice Scale Choice Why
Web/admin Existing Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind v4, Radix/shadcn primitives. Same, with deeper RBAC, audit components, and admin workflow primitives. Matches repo architecture and avoids rebuilding foundations.
Metadata Supabase Postgres with RLS, migrations, audit tables. Read replicas, partitioned audit/event tables, optional warehouse mirror. Postgres is the right control plane for operational state and trust evidence.
Object storage DigitalOcean Spaces with private buckets, signed URLs, path validation, checksums. S3-compatible multi-bucket topology, lifecycle tiers, cross-region backup. Large datasets belong in object storage, not Postgres.
Data processing Python 3.12 workers using Polars, PyArrow, DuckDB, Pandas where needed. Spark/Ray only for workloads that exceed single-node or DuckDB/Polars limits. Most pilots can be fast and cheap on columnar local compute before distributed processing.
File/table formats Parquet for tabular, JSONL for LLM/text, media objects plus metadata indexes. Delta Lake or Apache Iceberg for large mutable analytic tables after storage semantics are tested. Parquet/Arrow are universal; open table formats add ACID/time travel at higher operational cost.
Orchestration Postgres job registry plus worker queues for first implementation. Dagster assets with OpenLineage events and external worker execution. Start low-friction, then move repeatable dataset builds into an asset graph.
Data quality Custom scorecards plus Great Expectations/Pandera validation suites. Quality registry with trend dashboards, anomaly detection, and release blocking. Validation needs to become a release gate, not an ad hoc notebook.
PII/privacy Microsoft Presidio for text PII detection/anonymization, plus modality-specific scanners. Policy-driven DLP with reviewer queues, residual-risk scoring, and re-identification testing. PII detection must be built into ingestion and release gates.
Labeling Label Studio for task configuration, labeling, review, and annotation export. Integrated label ops with active learning, consensus scoring, and vendor management. Supports multiple modalities and avoids building a labeling UI from scratch.
Metadata standards Dataset Card, Croissant JSON-LD, SPDX license identifiers, release manifest. DCAT catalog mappings and W3C PROV/OpenLineage export. Improves portability, buyer review, catalog readiness, and auditability.

Why Not Put Data Processing In Next.js?

The web app should stay responsive and secure. Large file transforms, PII scans, labeling export imports, and package generation need isolated workers with explicit resource limits, resumable execution, structured logs, and no browser-exposed secrets.

Why Not Build A Marketplace First?

A marketplace without operational guarantees creates risk: unclear rights, inconsistent quality, stale listings, and buyer disappointment. The marketplace should be a projection of verified dataset products, not the source of truth.

State Machines

Dataset Build Stages

scoped -> rights_pending -> ingesting -> profiling -> cleaning -> pii_review -> labeling -> qa -> packaging -> delivery_ready -> delivered -> accepted

Release Approval States

draft -> qa_pending -> rights_pending -> privacy_pending -> approved -> delivered -> deprecated

Relational Design Rules

  • Use explicit status enums or constrained text values; do not infer status from missing fields.
  • Every high-risk approval needs approved_by, approved_at, approval_basis, and evidence_uri.
  • Every storage object referenced in metadata needs a checksum, byte size, content type, and retention policy.
  • Every worker-produced artifact links to the pipeline run, code version, input artifacts, and environment.
  • Use append-only audit events for rights, privacy, publication, pricing, delivery, and access changes.

Dataset Operations Pipeline

The pipeline should be modality-agnostic at the control-plane level and modality-specific in worker plugins. A tabular data build, image detection build, text corpus build, and multimodal build should all move through the same gates while using different processors.

01
Demand And SupplyCapture buyer briefs, supplier assets, commercial hypotheses, and fit scores.
02
Rights And RiskRecord ownership, consent, AI-use terms, privacy class, and blockers.
03
Ingest And ProfileLand raw data, fingerprint files, infer schemas, profile distributions.
04
Transform And LabelClean, normalize, enrich, anonymize, label, review, and split.
05
QA And DeliverScore, document, package, approve, transfer, and record acceptance.

Detailed Workflow

Stage Operator Actions System Outputs Release Gate
1. Qualification Score buyer need or supplier asset by use case, urgency, data scarcity, rights feasibility, budget, and build complexity. Structured brief or supplier asset, initial risk tier, project proposal, next action. Qualified owner and commercial hypothesis.
2. Feasibility Assess source availability, permitted uses, sample quality, volume, modality, processing cost, legal blockers, and delivery options. Feasibility memo, risk register, target schema, acceptance criteria, estimated timeline and cost. Go, pilot, pause, or reject decision.
3. Secure Intake Create data-room path, collect files/API credentials/share access, verify checksums, quarantine suspicious files. Ingestion run, raw artifacts, checksums, storage paths, source manifest. Raw data locked and source owner confirmed.
4. Profiling Run schema inference, type detection, missingness, duplicates, distributions, media metadata extraction, language detection, and volume checks. Schema profile, data profile, anomaly notes, recommended cleaning plan. Profile reviewed and target transformations approved.
5. Rights And PII Review Verify contracts, consent basis, field-level restrictions, PII classes, minimization opportunities, and labeling/export constraints. Rights review, privacy assessment, field policy map, redaction plan. Rights and privacy gates clear for the next stage.
6. Cleaning And Normalization Fix encoding, normalize units, standardize categories, deduplicate, remove corrupt items, align timestamps, repair malformed records. Silver artifacts, cleaning run logs, data diffs, rejected-record table. Validation checks pass or exceptions are approved.
7. Enrichment Add public signals, derived features, geocodes, taxonomy mappings, embeddings, weak labels, or entity normalization where useful. Enriched artifacts, enrichment provenance, source citations, confidence fields. Enrichment rights and quality reviewed.
8. Ontology And Labeling Define labels/classes, write labeling guidelines, create tasks, import pre-labels, assign annotators, review consensus and edge cases. Ontology version, label batches, annotation exports, reviewer decisions, label quality report. Label quality threshold met and guideline version frozen.
9. Split And Leakage Review Define train/validation/test split by entity/time/geography/source as appropriate; block duplicate or related entities across splits. Split manifest, leakage checks, class balance report, reproducible random seed. Split strategy approved for buyer use case.
10. Packaging Generate buyer-specific package, manifests, docs, previews, checksums, examples, and delivery notes. Release package, dataset card, Croissant JSON-LD, data dictionary, QA scorecard. Release approved by rights, privacy, QA, and commercial owners.
11. Delivery And Acceptance Provision signed URL/S3 share/API/warehouse delivery, verify checksum, track buyer download and acceptance. Delivery event, access log, acceptance evidence, support ticket link if needed. Accepted, remediated, or rejected with evidence.

Hard Release Blocks

  • Unknown or uncleared AI-training rights.
  • Unknown PII status or unapproved sensitive fields.
  • No release manifest or checksum coverage.
  • No data dictionary for tabular/text fields or no label ontology for supervised datasets.
  • Critical Great Expectations/Pandera checks failing without documented exception.
  • Unreviewed label batches below consensus threshold.
  • Buyer-specific restrictions not reflected in package contents.

Modality Playbooks

The control plane should stay consistent while processing plugins vary by data type. The platform must support tabular, text, image, video, audio, geospatial, time-series, and multimodal builds.

Modality Common Inputs Processing Focus Delivery Formats
Tabular / transactional CSV, Excel, database exports, warehouse shares, APIs. Schema inference, unit normalization, type repair, duplicate detection, joins, missingness, outliers, temporal leakage. Parquet, CSV, Arrow, DuckDB, SQL dump, warehouse share, JSONL for row-level ML tasks.
Text / documents Support tickets, PDFs, emails, knowledge bases, contracts, articles. OCR, encoding, language detection, document chunking, dedup, PII redaction, taxonomy labels, prompt/eval pairs. JSONL, Parquet, Markdown, TXT, prompt-completion JSONL, RAG corpus manifest.
Images Inspection photos, product images, satellite tiles, medical or industrial imagery. EXIF stripping/review, perceptual dedup, resolution normalization, bounding boxes, segmentation masks, class balance. COCO, YOLO, Pascal VOC, WebDataset shards, Parquet metadata plus object paths.
Video Dashcam, inspection, security, sports, manufacturing, field operations. Scene/clip extraction, frame sampling, temporal labels, blur/quality scoring, privacy masking, large-object packaging. MP4 clips plus JSONL/Parquet annotations, WebDataset, COCO-style frame annotations.
Audio / speech Calls, field recordings, voice notes, acoustic sensors. Transcription, diarization, language/accent metadata, PII redaction in transcripts, audio quality, speaker split leakage. WAV/FLAC/MP3 plus JSONL transcripts, timestamps, labels, speaker metadata.

Security, Privacy, And Trust

Caudals is selling trust in addition to data. Security and privacy controls must be visible in product state, enforced by backend gates, and backed by immutable evidence.

Access Model

Role Allowed Restricted
Admin Manage users, roles, settings, high-risk approvals. Direct raw data download without purpose and audit record.
Operator Manage leads, projects, assets, stage state, comments, delivery coordination. Rights/privacy approvals unless assigned reviewer permission.
Data engineer Run pipelines, inspect profiles, promote artifacts, view technical logs. Commercial pricing, contracts, unrelated supplier data.
Data scientist / QA Review quality, labels, splits, metrics, known limitations. Raw PII fields unless approved for the project.
Legal/privacy reviewer Approve or block rights and privacy assessments. Mutating pipeline artifacts or delivery packages.
Commercial ops Quotes, contracts, licenses, invoices, delivery acceptance, supplier share. Changing quality or privacy evidence.

Control Requirements

Data Access

  • RLS on all exposed metadata tables.
  • Service-role access only through scoped server wrappers.
  • Signed URLs with short TTLs and project-scoped authorization checks.
  • Object paths validated against known artifact records.
  • Raw and PII-bearing data access requires reason capture.

Privacy And PII

  • Run PII scans during ingestion and before labeling/export/release.
  • Separate anonymization, pseudonymization, redaction, encryption, and minimization decisions.
  • Treat pseudonymized data as still sensitive unless counsel determines otherwise.
  • Store salts/keys outside the repo and outside dataset packages.
  • Maintain field-level delivery policy for every package.

Provenance And Lineage

  • Track source, activity, agent, artifact, and derivation relationships.
  • Capture pipeline code version, environment, input checksums, output checksums.
  • Export OpenLineage-compatible run metadata when orchestration matures.
  • Use W3C PROV concepts for buyer-facing lineage summaries.

Release Assurance

  • Release packages require rights, privacy, QA, documentation, and commercial approval.
  • Every buyer delivery includes checksum verification and license summary.
  • Releases can be deprecated, revoked, or superseded with explicit reason.
  • Access logs and acceptance evidence remain linked to package version.

Risk Register Template

Risk Example Trigger Mitigation Release Decision
Unclear AI-training rights Supplier contract permits analytics but not model training. Block delivery until contract addendum or field removal. Hard block.
PII or sensitive data leakage Emails, faces, plates, GPS trails, identifiers remain in package. Minimize fields, redact/mask, aggregate, anonymize, or reject use case. Hard block unless approved residual risk exists.
Label noise Low reviewer agreement or Cleanlab-like issue detection flags many labels. Rework ontology, add gold set, increase consensus review. Block supervised training release above threshold.
Data leakage across splits Same entity, media sequence, or document family appears in train and test. Group-aware split, time-aware split, duplicate cluster split. Block model-eval package.
Supplier reputation risk Buyer-visible data reveals sensitive commercial behavior. Aggregate, delay, redact, segment package, or use private offer only. Reviewer decision required.

Migration tests pass; RLS policy tests cover admin/operator roles; legacy tables are not extended for new behavior.
Phase 40: Admin command center 2 weeks Private admin IA for leads, briefs, supplier assets, dataset build board, blockers, stage state, and audit activity. Operators can create and move a project from lead to scoped build with visible rights/PII/QA placeholders.
Phase 41: Secure ingestion and profiling 2-3 weeks Worker service, object storage layout, ingestion jobs, checksum manifests, schema/data profiling, preview generation, source manifests. A real pilot sample can be ingested, profiled, and reviewed without manual spreadsheet tracking.
Phase 42: Rights, privacy, and quality gates 2-3 weeks Rights review workflow, PII scan workflow, field policy map, quality expectations, scorecard, hard release blockers. No release candidate can be approved without rights, privacy, and quality evidence.
Phase 43: Labeling and curation ops 2-4 weeks Label Studio integration, ontology records, task export/import, reviewer queues, consensus metrics, label quality report. Image/text/tabular supervised pilot can complete labeling with review evidence.
Phase 44: Packaging and delivery 2-3 weeks Package generator, Dataset Card, Croissant JSON-LD, release manifest, data dictionary, signed delivery, buyer acceptance records. One production-ready package can be delivered with manifest, checksums, docs, license summary, and acceptance evidence.
Phase 45: Catalog/private offer readiness 3-5 weeks Private listing records, sample previews, pricing fields, refresh cadence, supplier revenue share, listing visibility gates. Internal team can prepare catalog-ready listings without exposing marketplace browse publicly.
Phase 46: Portal pilots Later Supplier intake and buyer request surfaces after internal workflows are proven. Public/self-serve surfaces only expose workflows that the internal platform can fulfill reliably.

MVP Backlog

Must Build Before First Production Delivery

  • New metadata schema and audit log.
  • Admin dataset build board.
  • Source/artifact storage registry with checksums.
  • Ingestion and profiling worker.
  • Rights and PII review records with release blockers.
  • Quality scorecard and release manifest.
  • Delivery event and buyer acceptance evidence.

Can Be Manual Initially

  • Legal document review, as long as the result is recorded.
  • Some cleaning/enrichment scripts, as long as outputs are registered as pipeline runs.
  • Specialist annotation vendor coordination, as long as batches and QA are tracked.
  • Commercial quotes, as long as package/license/delivery state is linked.

Engineering Acceptance Bar

  • Every phase includes migration tests, RLS/access tests, targeted unit tests, and admin UI checks.
  • Dataset-impacting changes include synthetic fixtures covering rights-approved, rights-blocked, PII-clear, and PII-blocked cases.
  • Worker jobs must be idempotent or safely retryable; failed jobs must leave recoverable metadata.
  • Every admin workflow must have explicit loading, empty, error, success, and blocked states.
  • Validation logs must include commands, screenshots for UI phases, and sample artifact metadata.

Metrics And Operating Model

The platform should make operational throughput and trust visible. Metrics should answer whether Caudals can deliver better datasets faster, with lower risk, and with repeatable margin.

Company Metrics

Category Metrics Why It Matters
Funnel Qualified buyer briefs, qualified supplier assets, reply time, discovery conversion, feasibility close rate. Shows demand/supply health and whether the public funnel is producing sellable opportunities.
Operations Cycle time by stage, blocker age, rework rate, on-time delivery, active projects per operator. Shows whether managed service work is becoming repeatable.
Quality QA pass rate, critical validation failures, duplicate rate, label agreement, data leakage incidents. Shows whether packages are trustworthy enough for buyer model work.
Trust Rights pass rate, PII block rate, unresolved privacy risk, release approvals without exceptions. Shows if Caudals is de-risking supply and protecting buyers/suppliers.
Commercial Feasibility-to-pilot conversion, pilot-to-complete conversion, gross margin by build, supplier revenue share, repeat/refreshed revenue. Shows whether operations create profitable reusable data products.

Operating Cadence

Daily Ops

  • Review active blockers and stage transitions.
  • Check failed pipeline runs.
  • Assign next actions for leads and builds.
  • Escalate rights or PII blockers.

Weekly Release Board

  • Review release candidates.
  • Compare quality score trends.
  • Approve or reject exceptions.
  • Update delivery forecasts.

Monthly Portfolio Review

  • Analyze buyer demand patterns.
  • Rank supplier assets by sellability.
  • Identify reusable catalog candidates.
  • Review margins and rework drivers.
