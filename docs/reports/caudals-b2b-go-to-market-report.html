Executive thesis

Start as a managed service, not a marketplace.

Caudals already has the platform foundations for requester briefs, supplier workflows, admin moderation, exports, payments, and support intake. The startup risk is not whether you can build screens. The risk is whether buyers will pay for a specific data outcome.

First milestone:
Book 15 buyer discovery calls, sell one paid feasibility sprint, and use that pilot to decide the next product automation.
Positioning

What Caudals sells

Caudals helps AI companies source, license, clean, document, and deliver high-quality business datasets from companies and public sources.

This is easier to buy than a generic dataset marketplace because the buyer has one acute problem: a model, agent, search system, or evaluation process is blocked by missing data.

Avoid early

Do not begin in the hardest categories

Avoid healthcare, children, biometrics, precise location, financial eligibility, raw personal communications, or other highly sensitive categories for the first pilots.

Begin with business datasets where rights, anonymization, and buyer value can be tested with lower operational risk.

The first version of Caudals should feel like a specialist data procurement team with a secure portal, not an empty catalog.

Buyer side

Custom dataset requests, feasibility assessment, sourcing, preprocessing, QA, documentation, and delivery.

Supplier side

Data assessment, anonymization review, licensing structure, approved-use controls, and revenue share.

Admin side

Lead pipeline, deal tracking, rights checklist, source registry, quality reports, export controls, and audit logs.

Buyer acquisition

The next 14 days are sales execution.

Your goal is to force a clear market answer quickly. A finished landing page is enough to begin. Build a focused lead list, send personalized outreach, and convert the strongest pain into paid feasibility sprints.

Output target:
100 buyer leads, 20 personalized emails per day, 15 discovery calls, 1 paid sprint.
Day 1
Choose the first wedge, write one buyer page, one supplier page, and one sample dataset card.
Days 2-3
Build a list of 100 buyers from LinkedIn, job posts, launch directories, AI company lists, funding news, and Hugging Face organizations.
Days 4-10
Send 20 personalized emails per day. Track reply rate, meeting rate, pain category, budget signal, and urgency.
Days 7-14
Run discovery calls. Ask for paid sprint commitment when the data problem is specific and urgent.

Subject: Dataset for [specific AI use case] Hi [Name], I saw [specific signal: your AI support launch / hiring for ML data / Spanish expansion]. I am building Caudals, a managed data procurement service for AI teams. We help companies source, license, clean, and document domain-specific datasets from business sources and public data. For [Company], I suspect [specific dataset] could improve [model, eval, or product outcome]. Supplier acquisition

Turn unused company data into controlled licensed inventory.

Do not ask suppliers to join a vague marketplace. Ask for a low-friction assessment of whether their operational data can become a controlled, licensed dataset.

Trust comes first:
Early suppliers should approve every buyer and every licensed use case until the process is proven.
Supplier targets

Where useful data lives

Ecommerce companies with product catalogs, FAQs, returns reasons, or category taxonomies.
BPO and support operations with anonymizable ticket taxonomies.
Industrial firms with manuals, maintenance logs, defects, or parts data.
Vertical SaaS companies with aggregated workflow data.
Marketplaces, associations, agencies, and data-rich SMEs.

Supplier qualification checklist

Caudals B2B Data Procurement Report 05
Operating model

Deliver the first pilots with a rigorous manual workflow.

Before investing in complex platform features, create a repeatable operating system for one dataset request. The software should mirror this workflow later.

Human approval stays mandatory:
Rights review, sensitive-data release, final buyer delivery, and commercial terms should not be fully automated.
01 Intake
Capture buyer use case, model need, data type, geography, language, timeline, budget, sensitivity, and acceptance criteria.
02 Brief
Translate the call into a dataset brief with target schema, source hypotheses, exclusions, quality rubric, and delivery format.
03 Source
Map suppliers and public sources. Contact suppliers with a specific ask tied to a real buyer use case.
04 Contract
Sign NDA, data evaluation agreement, supplier license, buyer license, and DPA where required.
05 Ingest
Receive data through secure upload, hash files, record source metadata, and isolate raw data from cleaned exports.
06 Process
Normalize, deduplicate, validate schema, scan for sensitive data, redact where approved, and create quality reports.
07 Deliver
Package the dataset, dataset card, schema, source inventory, QA report, and limitations statement.

Minimum delivery package

  • Cleaned dataset in JSONL, Parquet, and CSV if needed.
  • Schema and field dictionary.
  • Dataset card with intended use, limitations, provenance, and known risks.
  • Quality report with completeness, duplicates, distributions, and manual review notes.
  • License summary and approved-use restrictions.

Internal evidence to keep

  • Supplier permission and contract references.
  • Raw file hashes and processing logs.
  • Sensitive-data scan results and release approval.
  • Buyer acceptance criteria and sign-off.
  • Incident and takedown process.

Caudals B2B Data Procurement Report 07
Compliance posture

Make trust a product feature from day one.

Dataset procurement becomes risky when rights, personal data, intended use, or sublicensing are vague. Caudals should treat provenance and permitted use as first-class product data.

Not legal advice:
Get counsel before monetizing personal, sensitive, regulated, or high-risk datasets.
Track per dataset

Required dataset ledger fields

Source owner and commercial contact.
Permission to license, AI-training rights, and sublicensing restrictions.
Collection context and original terms.
Personal data, confidential data, IP, and sensitive category flags.
Approved uses, prohibited uses, retention rules, and deletion obligations.
Buyer-specific access history and export versions.
Contracts

Minimum legal pack

Mutual NDA for early samples.
Data evaluation agreement for pre-purchase review.
Supplier data license with rights warranties and approved uses.
Buyer data license with scope, restrictions, and no resale unless agreed.
DPA when personal data is involved.
Security exhibit for enterprise buyers.

Practical guardrails

Risk Default Caudals rule Evidence
Personal data Prefer non-personal or truly anonymized business data for first pilots. PII scan, human review, contract scope.
Re-identification Treat pseudonymized data as still risky unless counsel approves the use case. Redaction method, residual risk note.
Unauthorized AI training Do not accept supplier data unless AI use and sublicensing are explicitly covered. Supplier license and approved-use statement.
Buyer misuse Ban re-identification, unauthorized resale, and use outside the licensed purpose. Buyer license and audit logs.
Regulatory drift Review EU AI Act, GDPR, Data Governance Act, Data Act, and local rules for each category. Risk memo attached to dataset record.

Realistic pilots

Three examples you can sell.

Use these examples to make outreach concrete. Each one has a buyer pain, supplier path, deliverable, and pricing range.

Sales test:
If a prospect cannot name how the dataset improves a model, defer the pilot.
Example 01

Ecommerce product enrichment

Buyer: AI shopping/search startup.

Need: 50k Spanish product records with attributes, descriptions, taxonomy, and edge cases.

Sources: Retailers, PIM vendors, public catalogs.

Deliver: Normalized product data, taxonomy, duplicate detection, QA report.

Price: €5k sprint plus €18k pilot.

Example 02

Support-ticket intent dataset

Buyer: SaaS company training Spanish support agents.

Need: 20k anonymized tickets labeled by intent, urgency, and resolution type.

Sources: BPOs, ecommerce support teams, SaaS support operations.

Deliver: JSONL conversations, PII scan, label guide, eval split.

Price: €25k to €40k, with higher compliance burden.

Example 03

Industrial maintenance corpus

Buyer: Predictive maintenance AI company.

Need: Manuals, fault reports, maintenance logs, part descriptions.

Sources: Maintenance contractors, equipment distributors, public manuals.

Deliver: Clean document corpus, metadata, OCR where needed, retrieval/eval set.

Price: €20k to €60k depending on rights and processing.

What to say on the call

If they say... Respond with...
"We are not sure what data we need." "That is exactly the feasibility sprint. We turn your product goal into a concrete dataset spec and sourcing plan."
"Can you guarantee the data exists?" "Not before sourcing. We can guarantee the feasibility process, supplier outreach, risk map, and a go/no-go recommendation."
"We need this fast." "Then we should narrow scope to a pilot dataset with a clear acceptance test, not try to source the full production corpus first."
"We need personal data." "We can assess that, but the first step is legal basis, rights, minimization, anonymization, and buyer restrictions before any sample moves."

90-day plan

Move from calls to repeatable revenue.

The plan is intentionally commercial first. Product work happens in parallel, but only around the pipeline and delivery steps that make paid pilots easier to close.

North-star metric:
Paid dataset pipeline value, not waitlist count.
Week 1
Pick wedge, update landing CTAs, create buyer/supplier one-pagers, create sample dataset card, build 200-lead list.
Weeks 2-3
Run outbound, book discovery calls, qualify pain, ask for paid feasibility sprint, begin supplier outreach for repeated needs.
Weeks 4-5
Sell 1 to 2 feasibility sprints. Convert one into a pilot dataset build. Deliver manually with strong documentation.
Days 45-60
Automate intake scoring, brief generation, schema profiling, quality reports, and export packaging around the paid pilot.
Days 60-90
Publish two anonymized case studies, build supplier registry, create reusable templates, and raise prices after proof.

This week checklist

Choose the first wedge and write it on the landing page.
Replace generic waitlist CTA with "Request a dataset" and "Offer data".
Add budget, timeline, data type, geography, and sensitivity fields.
Create a simple buyer lead sheet and supplier lead sheet.
Send the first 40 personalized buyer emails.

Do not spend this month on

A public dataset marketplace with no supply.
Complex self-serve pricing before you know buyer willingness to pay.
A full CRM when a simple pipeline is enough.
High-risk regulated datasets before legal process is mature.
Contributor-scale features before supplier and buyer demand is validated.
